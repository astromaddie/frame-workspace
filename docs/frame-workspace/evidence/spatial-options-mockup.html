Spatial Mode: Two Ways to Make It Work

Mockups only. Last night showed one display per window is too much for macOS at the scale of everyday work (28 windows qualified). Both options keep everything already built: the Palladium chrome and handles, gaze focus with the glow and snap, cursor memory, bends, saved layouts, the dock and the switcher. Below each room view, how the Mac arranges the same windows behind the scenes.

Option A · Hybrid: Pull Windows Into the Room

Your Desktop stays as one large panel. Any window you pull off it becomes its own panel in the room; push it back and it rejoins the Desktop. Up to about eight windows live in the room this way.

ON THE MAC
In the Room
The Desktop panel in front, with its own Dock and menu bar; Terminal and Notes pulled out beside it, each its own panel with its menus. The room dock below lists everything, including what's still on the Desktop.
ON THE MAC
Pulling a Window Out
Grab a window's title bar on the Desktop panel and pull it towards you: it lifts out (blue outline where it was) and becomes its own panel where you let go. Pushing a panel into the Desktop panel puts it back.

Option B · Every Window, One Shared Display

No Desktop panel: every window is its own panel, as you wanted. On the Mac they all share one large hidden display instead of one display each, so the cost stays flat however many windows you have. The window you look at is raised to the front of that display, so your clicks and typing go to it.

ON THE MAC
In the RoomCLOSER TO YOUR IDEA
Every window floats on its own, Palladium chrome on each; the one you're looking at (Terminal) has focus. Its menus and popovers open on its panel.
ON THE MAC
Looking at Another Window
Look at Safari: its bezel glows and snaps, and on the Mac Safari comes to the front of the shared display, so the cursor and keyboard go to it. Panels in the room never move.
