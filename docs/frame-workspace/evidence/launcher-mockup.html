Spatial Mode: Launching Apps and System UI

Mockups only, for choosing before anything is built. Today the shelf opens pinned and running apps, but there's no way to start anything else, and Spotlight, permission prompts, open and save panels and notifications open on the covered laptop screen, where the headset can't see them. Same Palladium chrome as the approved sheets. Typing always happens on the Mac's own keyboard; the controllers point, the eyes pick.

1 · The Shelf: Placed by You, Magnified When You Reach for It

As decided today: you put the shelf where you want it and it stays pinned there. It's small at rest; when the laser or your eyes land on it, it grows smoothly, with the Mac Dock's magnification around the point you're at, and shrinks back a moment after you move on.

At Rest
Small and low, where you left it, with running apps marked and the status items at its right end. It doesn't follow your head; Recenter brings it back in front of you.
Laser or Eyes on It
The shelf grows to full size and the icons nearest the laser grow most, rising above it as on the Mac. With the eyes it's the same, centred where you look. It eases back to rest about half a second after you look or point away.
Moving It
While the laser is on the shelf, its grip slides out underneath, the same grip the windows use. Drag it to place the shelf anywhere in the room; it stays pinned there until you move it again.

2 · Launching Apps

Starting an app that isn't on the shelf. Each option can open from the shelf, from a controller button, or from Cmd-Space on the Mac keyboard (taken over from Spotlight while Spatial Mode runs). The panel opens at eye level in front of you; the windows behind it dim a little until it closes. Picking an app opens it and brings its window in front of you.

A · Applications Grid

Opens: the Applications button at the shelf's left end (laser click, or look at it and press a controller button), or Cmd-Space. A curved Launchpad-style grid with every app, pages swiped with the thumbstick, Recent Apps across the top.

Typing: the search field takes the Mac keyboard as soon as the grid opens; the grid narrows to matching apps as you type and Return opens the first.

Trade-offs: the best one for browsing and for controller-only use. It's a big panel over your work, and it shows apps only, no documents or folders.

B · Floating Search

Opens: Cmd-Space on the Mac keyboard, as on the Mac, or the Search button at the shelf's left end. A single field at eye level; results appear under it as you type.

Typing: keyboard first. Live results from the Mac's own Spotlight index: apps, then recent documents and folders. Arrow keys move, Return opens, Cmd-Return shows the file in Finder, Esc closes.

Trade-offs: the smallest and fastest, and it finds files too. Awkward with only the controllers, and you have to know what you're looking for.

C · Search That Grows Into the Grid: Opened

Opens: Cmd-Space or the shelf's Search button, as in B. It starts small: the field, then your Recent Apps underneath, ready for a laser click.

Typing: as in B: type and the recent apps give way to live results (apps, documents, folders); Return opens the top one.

C · Search That Grows Into the Grid: All Applications

Grows: Show All Applications (or the Down arrow on an empty field) unfolds the curved grid from A below the field, which stays where it was. Typing again narrows the grid.

Trade-offs: fast for the keyboard and complete for the controllers, with one button on the shelf. Two sizes of panel to build and get right, and the jump between them has to stay smooth.

3 · System UI

Spotlight, privacy and permission prompts, password prompts, open and save panels when they run outside the app, notifications and Control Center live outside any app window, so they open on the covered laptop screen. Two ways to bring them into the room, and the two together.

S1 · Each Surface Comes to You

How: whenever macOS shows a system surface, it appears on its own as a transient panel in front of you, takes focus, and goes away when it closes. Notifications sit small at the top right of your view and fade.

Trade-offs: nothing to learn. Each kind of surface has to be recognised and captured one by one; anything we miss stays invisible.

S2 · Laptop Screen Panel

How: a Laptop Screen button beside the status items (or a controller button) summons a panel showing the built-in display live, as it is under the black cover. Click and type into it like any window; press the button again to send it away.

Trade-offs: catches everything, including what we don't know about. You have to think to open it, and it's a whole screen for one small dialog.

S3 · Both: S1 First, S2 as the Safety NetRECOMMENDED

How: known surfaces come to you as in S1. If anything else opens on the laptop screen, the Laptop Screen button lights with a blue mark, so you know to look.

Trade-offs: nothing can hide; the work of S1 and S2 together, though S2 is small.
