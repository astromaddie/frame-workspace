The Menu Bar in Spatial Mode

In Spatial Mode every window sits on one hidden display on the Mac, so the focused app's menu bar (File, Edit, View…) isn't part of any window and needs a place of its own in the room. Its dropdown menus come with it, live. The menu bar always shows the app you're looking at; the cursor stays where it was in that window.

1 · App Menus

Where File, Edit, View… appear. The laser or the Mac's cursor opens a menu; it drops down from the bar as it does on the Mac.

M1 · Above the Focused WindowRECOMMENDED
A slim Palladium strip on the top bezel of the window you're looking at, with that app's menus. It moves to whichever window takes focus, so menus open right above what you're working on.
M2 · One Bar Overhead
One curved bar high in front of you, like the Mac's, showing the focused app's menus. It stays in place and follows you when you turn far away, like the shelf.
M3 · On the Shelf
The menus join the dock shelf below your line of sight: the app's menus on the left, its icons in the middle. Menus open upward. Everything that isn't a window is in one place.
M4 · Pull Down When Needed
No bar until you want it: a small tab sits on the focused window's top bezel; point at it (or press Cmd-?) and the menus slide out like the adjustment handles, then tuck away.

2 · Status Items

The right-hand side of the Mac's menu bar: the clock, Wi-Fi, battery, Control Center and app icons such as ExpressVPN. Clicking one opens its menu or panel as on the Mac.

I1 · Right End of the ShelfRECOMMENDED
Status items sit at the shelf's right end, past the dock icons, as they sit at the right of the Mac's screen. Always there, never in front of your work.
I2 · With the Menus
Status items stay on the right of the menu bar, wherever the menus go (with M1 they ride along above the focused window).
I3 · Behind One Button
Only the clock shows, on the shelf; pointing at it fans out every status item above it.
